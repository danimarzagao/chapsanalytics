Dear Editors of the Journal of Business Research,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "The Scarcity Heuristic in Hypermodern Consumption: Why the Hunter-Gatherer Brain Migrated from Object to Existential Experience," for consideration for publication in the Journal of Business Research.

This theoretical article addresses a question that luxury marketing researchers have described but not fully explained: why has the contemporary consumer abandoned material possession as a primary status signal in favor of time, experience, and existential authenticity? We argue that this transition is not merely a cultural shift, but a predictable adaptive response by a brain calibrated for scarcity now operating in material abundance. Drawing on costly signaling theory, consumer neuroscience (mesolimbic dopaminergic system, orbitofrontal cortex), and the philosophy of value, we propose the Multidimensional Perceived Scarcity (MPS) model as an integrative framework.

The manuscript contributes to JBR's focus on consumer psychology and behavior by providing a mechanistic explanation — grounded in evolutionary biology and neuroscience — for a phenomenon that marketing and management research have so far described only descriptively.
